# Figure captions

**Figure 1 | Original failures are numerical, while the corrected formulation completes the same reference problem.** Internal accepted step, largest error-weighted state component, cumulative Jacobian count and minimum liquid-cell length are shown for original strict tolerances, a full-inventory error-weight ablation and the improved reference case. Crosses mark actual failures. All curves shown here use the same 1 nm initial liquid thickness; numerical formulations differ as labelled. The panel displays internal times through 1000 s. The weighted RMS acceptance norm is not the largest component alone.

**Figure 2 | Early interface fluxes converge without discarding an arbitrary first hour.** Crust-side diffusion, liquid-side diffusion and contact concentration offset are compared at 128/256/512 crust cells and two time settings for u=.6 and x008. Solid/dashed curves denote baseline/refined time settings. Both fluxes must differ by less than 1% Sref at subsequent common samples; tables give absolute errors and supported windows. Positive flux is downward. Zero time is omitted on logarithmic axes, retained in source data. These are deterministic results, not replicate means.

**Figure 2b | The first two seconds of interface motion remain in the comparison.** Top, bottom and geometric net velocities use the same spatial/time comparisons as Figure 2. Only the displayed interval is 0–2 s; complete trajectories remain in the source data. The early concentration relaxation can be faster than the linear-axis display scale.

**Figure 2c | Seed thickness and initialization change the physical transient.** The top row compares 0.1/1/10 nm seeds at 512 cells. The bottom row compares uniform and stationary-concentration initial conditions at a fixed 1 nm seed. Each initial condition has its own conserved initial dissolved inventory. These differences are not labelled numerical error.

**Figure 3 | ALE predicts two independent geometric interfaces.** Actual top position, bottom position and their difference are shown for all five primary forcing cases. No ALE height is inferred using analytical phi_core. Dashed curves in the thickness panel independently integrate the corrected net law from the same initial geometric thickness; all time-dependent forcing knots are included in the integration. Constant cases end at 50 h; x008 ends at the supported 44.70875 h.

**Figure 4 | Top and net growth are separate analytical hypotheses.** Solid curves are actual ALE speeds; dashed curves are the unmodified finite-thickness top law and thick-crust net law. Lower panels show signed deviations normalized by the nonzero reference velocity. The initial uniform field lacks the stable-core/quasi-steady structure assumed by the analytical laws; full histories are nevertheless retained to make their limited transient scope visible.

**Figure 5 | A small net discrepancy can result from cancellation.** Signed top error, diagnostic inferred-bottom discrepancy and their net difference are shown separately. The analytical bottom speed is top prediction minus net prediction, not a separately validated interface law. The u=.9 endpoint cancellation is quantified in the report.

**Figure 6 | Bottom kinetics affects the growth path more strongly than the late speed.** Actual bottom speed, bottom position and crust thickness are compared for kappa=.5/1/2 at 256 cells and identical physical times. Rows correspond to u=.6/.9. No kappa is selected by closeness to the target law. Tables report peak transient sensitivity as well as endpoint differences.

**Figure 7 | Birth, mean, core and bottom-trace solid fractions are different observables.** Volume mean, core-region mean, final spatial profiles and front trace are independently reconstructed. The dashed theoretical constant is not an ALE input. Core means use partial-cell overlap with the region between three reference reaction lengths from each boundary; absent initial cores are NaN. Stable-looking late values do not isolate the causal contribution of variable area versus diffusivity.

**Figure 8 | Finest-grid agreement is assessed over common time histories.** Actual top/bottom/net speeds and geometric thickness are compared for 128→256 and 256→512, using matched settings and saved times. Velocity differences use a nonzero reference; thickness differences use the finer actual thickness. The dotted 1% line is a numerical target, not an experimental model-accuracy criterion. Tables additionally contain u=0/.3 finest-pair comparisons and absolute differences.

**Figure 8b | Strict temporal agreement is independently checked.** The three representative 128-cell cases and an additional 512-cell u=.6 case compare tighter tolerances/step limits. Exactly zero differences are retained in the tables and omitted only from the logarithmic ordinate. Temporal agreement does not validate the bottom exposure law.
