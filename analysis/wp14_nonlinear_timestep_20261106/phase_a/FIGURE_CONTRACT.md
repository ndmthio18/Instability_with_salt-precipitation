# WP14 Phase A figure contract

Backend: Python/matplotlib only. Archetype: quantitative grid. Final width: 180 mm. Exports: editable PDF/SVG, 300 dpi PNG preview, reproducible 600 dpi TIFF. All panels use the full frozen data relevant to their stated comparison; no case was dropped.

## Figure 1

Core conclusion: amplitude normalization reduces but does not eliminate the earlier nonlinear development at larger initial amplitude.

- Top row: full `R_harm(t)` evolution for all nine trajectories.
- Bottom row: predefined `R_harm=0.01/0.1` registered crossings versus the original absolute H23 milestone. The third predefined level, `R_harm=1`, is retained in the source table and report but omitted from the artwork to keep the timing comparison legible.

## Figure 3

Core conclusion: the main resolved positive transport pulse is more stable than the first weak positive interval, particularly for `A=1e-4` at 5--10 mm.

- Rows: three amplitudes kept separate.
- Columns: 1, 2, 5 and 10 mm.
- Thin lines: all resolved positive intervals (`J_adv>1e-15 m/s`).
- Thick segments: largest-positive-integral contiguous pulse.
- Blank panels mean no resolved positive flux, not zero transport.

## Figure 4

Core conclusion: adjacent-grid differences decrease most consistently for the enhanced-amplitude R48/R96 pair, while the baseline and intermediate amplitude retain branch- or signal-resolution limitations.

- Each amplitude has its own panel; amplitudes are never pooled.
- `N/A` means the timing or positive signal is unresolved under the registered floor.
- Percentages are finite-resolution differences relative to the finer grid, not convergence rates or error bounds.
