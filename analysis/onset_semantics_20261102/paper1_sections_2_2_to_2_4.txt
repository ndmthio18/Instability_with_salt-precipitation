The competing precipitation time ˆtprecip is calculated from the same base

NaCl solubility limit of 0.0977 mol mol−1 (26 wt%) used by Bringedal et al.

three-month period of sustained evaporation under constant forcing. This
gives a case-specific dimensionless limit ˆt90 = TE/(ϕH). Each case receives
one of four mutually exclusive outcomes:
• Instability first. The critical crossing occurs before precipitation and
within the horizon, ˆtcrit < min(ˆtprecip, ˆt90).
• Precipitation first. The surface reaches the solubility limit before the
base state becomes unstable and within the observation horizon.
• Horizon censored. Neither event occurs within 90 days, and the steady-
state analysis does not identify a stable limiting base state; the onset
time is right-censored at the horizon.
• Never unstable. Neither event occurs within 90 days, and the steady
base state remains linearly stable, with its critical Rayleigh number
exceeding RE [17]. This structural status is distinct from horizon cen-
soring.
Primary first-event labels are assigned by comparing critical onset, precipi-
tation and the observation horizon, with steady-state stability distinguishing
never-unstable cases. Events within the horizon take precedence over the
steady-state classification; coincident instability and precipitation times are
assigned to precipitation first. Onset-time regression is restricted to cases
classified as instability first. The regression targets are dimensionless, with
physical time recovered as t = ˆt ϕH/E.
