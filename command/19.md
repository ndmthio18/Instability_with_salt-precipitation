## Task 3 — Main scientific analysis: Grid convergence of downward salt transport

Compare the existing:

- WP10 R24-M1 long-time simulation
- WP11 R48-M1 25T simulation

\[
dt=113.68588647695695\ \mathrm{s}.

### A. Downward advective salt flux

For each of the original observation planes at approximately 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 mm, analyze:

\[
J_{\mathrm{adv}}(z,t).
\]

Downward is positive.

Report:

- First positive flux time;
- First time exceeding the registered numerical threshold;
- Longest continuous positive-flux interval;
- Total number and duration of positive accepted steps;
- Maximum positive flux;
- Time of peak flux;
- Positive-flux time integral;
- Signed cumulative advective flux.

Use the existing threshold \(10^{-15}\) m/s as a consistent numerical screen, but assess whether the identified signals are also distinguishable from actual numerical uncertainty.

### B. Total salt flux

For the same depths, calculate:

\[
J_{\mathrm{total}}=J_{\mathrm{adv}}+J_{\mathrm{diff}}.
\]

Separate:

- Advective transport;
- Diffusive transport;
- Net total transport.

Do not interpret positive total flux as proof of net downward convection if the advective component remains negative.

Compute both positive-only and signed cumulative fluxes.

### C. Grid convergence of transport quantities

Compare R24/R48/R96 at matching physical times and over identical integration windows.

Priority metrics:

1. First sustained downward advective transport;
2. Peak downward advective flux;
3. Integrated positive advective transport;
4. Signed cumulative total transport;
5. Salt inventories above each observation plane;
6. Depth reached by net downward transport.

Quantify R24–R48 and R48–R96 differences.

Do not simply judge convergence by whether the flux signs agree.

If R48 and R96 are substantially closer than R24 and R48, report this as evidence of improving consistency, not proof of asymptotic convergence.

Do not use a Richardson extrapolation unless the numerical data genuinely support the required assumptions.
