## Scientific motivation

We have reviewed Tilton & Riaz (2014), *Nonlinear stability of gravitationally unstable, transient, diffusive boundary layers in porous media*.

An important insight is that nonlinear interactions can generate a horizontally uniform (zero-wavenumber) response, even when the initial perturbation consists of a single nonzero Fourier mode.

For Paper 2, the central question is:

**When does evaporation-driven density instability begin to produce a physically meaningful modification of the horizontally averaged salt distribution and conservative salt transport, and does this happen before the local solubility threshold is reached?**

The current R24/R48 results show substantial late-time grid sensitivity. Therefore, do not interpret the large R24 perturbation as validated physical convection.

The immediate objective is to extract as much reliable information as possible from existing simulations before launching another expensive calculation.

For each two-dimensional solution, calculate the horizontal mean:

\[
\bar{x}(z,t)=\frac{1}{W}\int_0^W x(x,z,t)\,dx.
\]

Clearly separate the following three comparisons:

**A. Seeded versus unseeded 2D**

\[
\Delta\bar{x}_{seed}
=\bar{x}_{M1}-\bar{x}_{M0}.
\]

This describes sensitivity to the prescribed initial perturbation. It is not automatically the effect of convection relative to a convection-free background.

**B. Unseeded 2D versus 1D background**

\[
\Delta\bar{x}_{unseeded}
=\bar{x}_{M0}-x_b.
\]

This describes how the unseeded two-dimensional trajectory departs from the horizontally uniform reference.

**C. Seeded 2D versus 1D background**

\[
\Delta\bar{x}_{conv}
=\bar{x}_{M1}-x_b.
\]

This is a candidate mean-response measure, not a validated causal convection contribution until the 1D baseline and the numerical errors are verified.

## Task 2 — Analyze the zero and nonzero Fourier modes separately

For R24 and R48, construct the following diagnostics over the full existing simulation:

1. Horizontally averaged concentration profiles;
2. Horizontal perturbation fields relative to each run's own mean;
3. Original canonical mode amplitude;
4. Higher-harmonic mode amplitudes;
5. Spectral energy distribution;
6. Energy-weighted characteristic physical wavenumber.

Define the spectral peak:

\[
k_{peak}(t)=\arg\max_{k>0}E(k,t)
\]

and energy-weighted wavenumber:

\[
k_E(t)=
\frac{\sum_{k>0}kE(k,t)}
{\sum_{k>0}E(k,t)}.
\]

Use the physical wavenumber rather than only the discrete mode index.

Keep the distinction between:

- Nonzero-mode perturbation energy;
- The horizontal-mean concentration response;
- The mean profile of the evolving background.

The existing Fourier energy excludes the horizontal mean by construction. Do not interpret it as the energy of the full perturbation relative to the 1D state.

Compare R24/R48 from T to 4T, emphasizing the divergence after 3T.

Do not claim that the onset of higher harmonics proves nonlinear convection without numerical and physical validation.
