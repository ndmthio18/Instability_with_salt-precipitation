## 2. Phase A — Independent reanalysis of existing nine trajectories

Do not rerun DuMux.

Use the frozen WP12 datasets and archived field outputs.

### Task A1 — Amplitude-independent nonlinear diagnostics

The previous nonlinear milestone was defined as:

H23 = sqrt(harmonic2_top² + harmonic3_top²) >= 1e-5

This is an absolute-amplitude criterion.

Its occurrence time may shift simply because the initial perturbation amplitude changes.

Develop additional normalized diagnostics that measure nonlinear harmonic development relative to the seeded mode.

The initial seeded mode is the physical A28 mode, corresponding to m=2 in the reduced box.

For example, calculate:

R_harm(t) = sqrt[E_nonseed(t) / E_seed(t)]

where:

E_seed = E_2

E_nonseed = sum of E_m for m != 2, m >= 1.

Also separately calculate:

- Even harmonic energy fraction;
- Odd-mode energy fraction;
- m=4, m=6 and m=10 energy fractions;
- Total nonzero perturbation energy;
- Mirror-symmetry error.

Specify the Fourier basis, vertical integration weights and normalization.

Avoid false thresholds when the seeded energy is extremely small.

Show several predefined nonlinear energy ratios, but do not optimize thresholds to obtain apparent agreement.

For each amplitude and grid, calculate the time at which the normalized nonlinear harmonic ratio crosses the selected thresholds and remains above them for a registered duration.

Compare these normalized milestones with the previous absolute H23 milestone.

Determine whether the reported 45–47% earlier nonlinear onset at A=1e-4 remains meaningful under normalized criteria.

### Task A2 — Nonlinear mode-selection audit

Analyze the temporal evolution of:

- m=2;
- m=4;
- m=6;
- m=10;
- Odd modes;
- Total perturbation energy.

Compare all three amplitudes at:

1. Identical physical times;
2. Identical normalized nonlinear-harmonic levels, when those levels are reached;
3. The previously registered t_nl + 1T, retained only as an auxiliary comparison.

Do not align time histories by fitting arbitrary shifts.

Investigate why:

- A=1e-6 is dominated by m=4 at 6T;
- A=1e-5 is dominated by m=6;
- A=1e-4 is dominated by m=2.

Distinguish physically generated harmonics from possible numerical noise amplification.

In particular, investigate whether the marked suppression of odd-mode energy at A=1e-4 explains its improved R48/R96 consistency.

Do not assume causality from correlation alone.

### Task A3 — Physically meaningful downward-transport timing

The previous positive-flux criterion identifies the first 50 consecutive accepted steps with:

J_adv > 1e-15 m/s

But the earliest weak positive-flux interval may not represent the dominant transport event.

For each of the observation planes at 1, 2, 5 and 10 mm, calculate:

1. First resolved positive net advective flux;
2. First sustained positive-flux interval;
3. Largest positive-flux pulse;
4. Peak positive-flux time;
5. Pulse duration;
6. Positive advective flux integral;
7. Signed advective flux integral;
8. Signed total salt flux integral.

Additionally define a cumulative positive-flux fraction:

F(z,t) = C_adv+(z,t) / C_adv+(z,6T)

For cases with C_adv+(z,6T) above the registered resolvability floor, report:

- t_05: 5% of the final positive integral;
- t_10: 10%;
- t_50: 50%;
- t_90: 90%.

The denominator must always use the same frozen 0–6T observation window.

If no resolved positive flux exists, label these quantities undefined rather than zero.

These are transport-pulse timing diagnostics, not a validated physical t_eff.

### Task A4 — Quantify convergence without conflating amplitudes

For each amplitude separately, compare:

R24 → R48 → R96.

Calculate adjacent-grid differences for:

- Normalized nonlinear milestone time;
- Surface mean peak concentration and time;
- Surface concentration decline;
- 5 mm and 10 mm main positive-flux pulse timing;
- Peak advective salt flux;
- Positive advective integral;
- Signed total integral;
- Mirror-symmetry error;
- Dominant-mode fractions.

Assess whether adjacent-grid differences decrease under refinement.

Never claim Richardson convergence or a numerical order of accuracy from inconsistent nonlinear branches.

## 5. Error-budget interpretation

Use the existing R48 and R96 dt simulations to quantify the horizontal-resolution difference:

ΔQ_x = |Q_R96,dt - Q_R48,dt|

Use the new R48 dt/2 run to quantify the temporal-resolution difference:

ΔQ_t = |Q_R48,dt/2 - Q_R48,dt|

Report their ratio for each quantity where the comparison is meaningful.

Interpretation:

- If ΔQ_t is much smaller than ΔQ_x, horizontal-grid effects remain the larger observed source of numerical variation for that QoI.
- If ΔQ_t is comparable to or greater than ΔQ_x, spatial consistency cannot be interpreted independently of timestep error.
- If the modal branches differ, do not infer formal convergence order.

These are finite-resolution differences, not rigorous upper bounds on truncation error.

Do not assume spatial and temporal errors are additive or independent without further testing.
