## 1. Objective

Repository: `ndmthio18/surrogate_model`  
Branch: `codex/paper2-main`  
Reference commit: `427fc703754ed53e06b300c14751714348463bfa`

We have completed WP10, a long-time R24-M1 exploratory simulation for Case 36877.

The R24 simulation stopped at 21.2516667T after the first local solubility-threshold crossing.

The key observations were:

- Local downward flow became visible around 5T.
- Net downward advective salt flux occurred temporarily at approximately 1–5 mm depth during 4–6T.
- Net downward total salt flux reached approximately 10 mm.
- Surface mean salt concentration decreased after the onset of strong nonlinear instability.
- The first local salt mole fraction reached 0.1 at 21.25T.
- The R24 nonlinear evolution has not been shown to be grid-converged.

**The goal is now to run R48-M1 and R96-M1 concurrently on HPCC and establish whether these physical observations persist under horizontal refinement.**

Importantly, R48 results should be analyzed as soon as that job finishes, without waiting for R96 to finish.

## 3. HPCC execution strategy

Use the hpcc `cnmix` partition.

**Submit two independent production jobs so that R48 and R96 can execute concurrently.**

Each job should:

- Use one compute node and one Slurm task.
- Benchmark 1 versus 4 TBB threads on its own grid.
- Select the appropriate thread count based on measured cost.
- Use node-local scratch where available.
- Stage large compressed archives to persistent storage.
- Preserve executable, input and profile SHA-256 hashes.
- Enforce an explicit wall-clock and storage budget.
- Record the Slurm job ID and resource usage.
- Stop immediately at the first accepted local x >= 0.1 event.

Check the actual HPCC file-count quota, storage availability, partition limits and system load before submission.

Do not use a default wall limit copied from R24. Estimate R48 and R96 independently.

The R96 workload may become substantially more expensive during nonlinear development; resource planning must account for that possibility.

## 5. R96 completion and final three-grid comparison

After R96 finishes, independently validate it.

Produce the final report:

`WP11 — Stage B: R24/R48/R96 Long-Time Comparison`

Compare the three simulations at identical physical times where all results are available.

### A. Downward-flow onset

Evaluate local downward velocity at matching saved frames.

Verify the VTK cell connectivity and correct concentration–velocity spatial mapping.

Report:

- First observed downward-flow time;
- Downward-flow area fraction;
- Peak downward Darcy velocity;
- High-concentration/downward-velocity spatial correlation;
- Finger penetration depth.

Cell-centre velocity is qualitative and must not be presented as a conservative face flux.

### B. Conservative salt transport

For the original seven planes, compare:

- Signed advective salt flux;
- Signed diffusive salt flux;
- Signed total salt flux;
- Cumulative advective and diffusive transport;
- Regional salt inventory;
- Conservation residuals.

For positive net flux intervals, report:

1. First positive accepted step;
2. Last positive accepted step;
3. Number of actually positive steps;
4. Longest continuous positive interval;
5. Peak positive flux;
6. Time integral of positive net-flux contributions.

Do not treat the entire span between first and last positive steps as continuously positive.

The original solver provides plane-integrated net fluxes, not the spatially separated positive/negative local SCVF contributions. Keep these concepts separate.

### C. Surface salt accumulation

Compare:

- Surface mean concentration;
- Surface maximum concentration;
- Maximum domain concentration;
- Surface concentration standard deviation;
- First local solubility-threshold event bracket.

Use the same concentration threshold and event-detection semantics for all three simulations.

### D. Nonlinear mode selection

Calculate the full physical-wavenumber spectrum.

Track at least m=1, 2, 4, 6 and 10.

Pay particular attention to the under-resolved m=10 mode in R24 and whether R48/R96 reproduce its energy growth and later modal transitions.

### E. Timestep sensitivity

Compare the new R96 simulation with the existing WP9 R96 dt/2 simulation over 0–4T.

Keep this timestep sensitivity separate from the R48/R96 horizontal-grid comparison.

If temporal differences are already important at 4T, do not interpret late differences as solely horizontal-grid errors.

## 8. GO/NO-GO decision

Distinguish three possible outcomes:

**Outcome A — Mechanism reproduced**

R48 and R96 both show downward salt transport with reasonably consistent timing, depth and magnitude.

Then propose a more precise conservative local-flux mechanism analysis, followed by necessary temporal and vertical-resolution checks.

**Outcome B — Mechanism persists, but its quantitative properties remain sensitive**

Downward transport occurs in both refined simulations, but timing, depth or strength differ substantially.

Report a plausible physical mechanism without assigning a grid-independent transport onset or solubility-delay magnitude.

**Outcome C — Mechanism disappears under refinement**

The R24 downward transport window is absent or becomes numerically insignificant at finer grids.

Treat the R24 long-time result as potentially affected by under-resolved short waves and reassess the physical interpretation.

In all cases, do not claim actual precipitation kinetics or validated crystallization delay.
